## How Japan's holiday rules work

Two rules create extra days off that many calendars miss.

The first is the **substitute holiday** (振替休日). When a national holiday falls on a Sunday, the next weekday that isn't already a holiday becomes a day off.

The second is the **citizens' holiday** (国民の休日). Any weekday sandwiched between two national holidays automatically becomes a holiday too. That's what created the five-day Silver Week in September 2026.

The equinox holidays are the only ones not fixed in advance by law. Their dates depend on astronomical calculations, and the government announces them each February for the following year.

## What the holidays celebrate

Several holidays are tied to Mondays rather than fixed dates, under what's known as the Happy Monday System. Coming of Age Day, Marine Day, Respect for the Aged Day and Sports Day all fall on a set Monday, which guarantees a three-day weekend.

Coming of Age Day honors young people entering adulthood, with ceremonies held by local governments. Japan lowered the legal age of adulthood to 18 in 2022, but many ceremonies still invite 20-year-olds.

Showa Day on April 29 was the birthday of Emperor Showa. It now opens Golden Week, which runs through Constitution Memorial Day, Greenery Day and Children's Day in early May.

Marine Day gives thanks for the ocean, and Mountain Day, added in 2016, celebrates the mountains. Sports Day was renamed from Health and Sports Day in 2020.

The Emperor's Birthday follows the reigning emperor. It has fallen on February 23 since the current emperor took the throne in 2019.

## Busy periods travelers should know

Three periods affect prices and crowds more than any other: New Year, Golden Week and Obon.

The New Year period isn't just January 1. Many companies close from around December 29 to January 3, and domestic travel peaks around those dates.

Golden Week is the busiest travel period of spring. Trains, flights and hotels in popular areas fill up early, and prices rise accordingly.

Obon, usually around August 13–16, isn't a national holiday. But many people take leave then to visit family, so long-distance trains and highways are packed.

## Banks, offices and shops on holidays

Japanese banks close on national holidays and over the New Year from December 31 to January 3. Convenience store ATMs keep working, which is the easiest way to get cash during those days.

Government offices and most companies follow the same pattern. Department stores, restaurants and tourist attractions usually stay open on national holidays, though some close for a few days over New Year.

## Planning a trip around the holidays

If your dates are flexible, avoid arriving at the start of Golden Week or Obon. Popular sights get very crowded, and you'll pay more for the same hotel room.

Three-day weekends are less dramatic, but they still raise demand in hot-spring towns and areas within easy reach of Tokyo and Osaka. Booking a refundable room early is the safe move.

Holidays can also work in your favor. Big business hotels in central Tokyo or Osaka sometimes get quieter on holidays, when business travel drops off.
